# Legacy TRAIN320: independent semantic audit

The legacy packet is factually consistent, but most unique priorities are not established by the visible request. All 320 rows were covered through their complete 20-situation grid, all 80 full backgrounds, both browsing-query sessions, original/contrast histories and separate labels. This audit read no model predictions or future evaluation and changed no data.

## What the full review establishes

The actual public input formatter accepts all 320 requests. All 2,360 store-history facts match the frozen sixteen-product catalog; 160 additional events are explicitly external. All positive product prices and all owned-product exclusions pass. There are no typed hard requirements or explicit replacement requests in these 320 rows. All ten manifest-pinned files remain unchanged.

There are 320 distinct model-visible public contexts, including after absolute history dates are removed. No conflicting labels for identical visible input were found; there are also no exact duplicate contexts, so this is not proof that the subjective rankings are identifiable. Outer profile/session/event IDs are excluded by the actual formatter. No protected demographic or person identity field supplies a hidden priority: four generic home/routine suffixes, budgets and palette families vary while each situation’s functional grade vector stays fixed.

The 320 rows contain only 20 situation templates, crossed with four generic context/palette/budget variations and two browsing queries, then doubled by ownership contrasts. Neither query nor any background states an explicit priority order. A described activity is not proof that equipment for it is missing. The grading remains authored synthetic recommendation policy, not observed shopper preference.

## Ownership contrast is sound; inherited priority is less certain

Every original grade-3 target is absent from that original’s observed history (160/160). All 160 contrasts replace an external **purchase** with a purchase of that annotated target. The event type never changes. All newly owned products then receive grade 0, and all 176 former grade-2 bindings are promoted to grade 3. Sixteen contrast rows have two maximum products; the other 144 have one. This is an authored ownership intervention, not a fixed-product view-to-purchase experiment.

All contrast maxima are source-valid and unowned. Of their 176 maximum product bindings, 170 have observed view/cart interest and six rely on context alone. Public interest can support an optional recommendation; it does not by itself establish that product as the unique next priority. Repeated view/cart products also receive grade 0 in 139 eligible, unowned product bindings in each half of the packet, so there is no consistent engagement-to-grade rule to recover from the data. Grade 0 remains “outside these authored priorities,” not universal irrelevance.

Concrete examples use the full ID prefix `history-u-` and suffix `-purchase-contrast-v2`:

- `4c5be51ba3a0-s2`: after tote purchase, a bottle is promoted without a bottle observation. Refill-shop visits and pantry-jar purchases do not specify a drinking-water refill need.
- Picnic and bedside contrasts each contribute eight two-way maxima. Those sets preserve indifference; consumers must not select one as uniquely preferred.

## Assessment of the proposed loss change

Using frozen-base KL on the original 160 would remove their hard-label assumption. It would **not** certify all 160 contrast CE targets: those targets inherit private grade-2 ordering rather than gaining an observable next-need priority. The contrast review contains 16 stronger problem/goal leaders, 128 inferred unique priorities and 16 tied maxima. There are no wrong-owned, price or replacement labels; the uncertainty is the identity and relative importance of the next unmet need.

The proposed fixed objective ablation can test a declared research hypothesis. It must retain this uncertainty and all current development outcomes; this audit does not predict improvement. Under that plan, 160 of the 288 CE profiles (55.56%) would still use legacy contrast labels; actual gradient weight depends on the chosen loss weights. The original legacy320 comprise 71.43% of the combined448 TRAIN rows, which is a data-composition fact, not a causal explanation for the measured regression.

A more directly observable use of these contrasts would teach demotion of the purchased product while preserving uncertainty over eligible unowned alternatives, or keep weak/set-valued complement supervision until a separately versioned request explicitly states the unmet need and relative priority. These are proposed objectives, not measured results. Preserve the frozen data and failed runs, and do not retarget evaluation.

The four broad palette families also do not establish a uniquely explicit canonical shade. This audit checks target variants exist and belong to positive products; it does not certify every shade as uniquely preferred. Likewise, ordinary carrying or outdoor use does not establish laptop dimensions, UV ratings or outdoor-performance product guarantees.
